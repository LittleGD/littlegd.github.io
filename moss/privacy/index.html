Moss’s Club house Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 16, 2026

Moss’s Club house (the “app”) is made by Jongmin Lee, an independent developer at JML.Studio (“we”). This policy explains what information the app uses, why, who helps us process it and how long we keep it.

Summary

  • Other users never see your name or email. They see your letter and an approximate region.
  • Letters and replies are sent to OpenAI for safety review and translation. OpenAI does not write them. A letter to your future self goes to no one else. Its text is sent to the external AI service to get a question, without a press from you, when you stop at the end of a sentence or paragraph while the question switch is on (it is on by default, and turning it off stops this). A summary card or a lookback is sent only when you ask for one.
  • Your approximate city comes from your network connection. The app doesn’t use GPS.
  • If you report being 19 or older, rewarded ads may be personalized or general. If you are 18 or younger, you can claim one spark a day by checking in.
  • Apple or Google handles payment for a paid membership. We do not receive payment details such as your card number.
  • You can delete your account and letters in the app at any time.

1. Information we collect

Account. When you sign in with Apple or Google, we receive an account identifier and your email address (Apple may give us a private relay address). We don’t store your name or profile photo from these providers. If you use Sign in with Apple, we also keep an encrypted Apple token so we can revoke your Apple sign-in when you delete your account.

Profile. Your app language, your time zone, your approximate city, the version of the terms you accepted, when you were last active, and the age group you choose (18 or younger, or 19 or older). The app asks your age group once: when you put your first new worry letter or encouragement letter in its envelope, on the desk when no letters have arrived, or at the shop’s daily reward. We do not ask for a birth date.

Letters and replies. The text of letters and replies you write, their translations, review results, thank-you notes, and delivery details such as dates and which letters you were offered or chose.

Letters to your future self. The text of the letters you write, the questions that were printed on the paper when you sent them, when and on which day you sent them, the return day you chose, when the arrival notice and the early notice were sent, when you read them, the passages chosen for summary cards and lookbacks, the kind and period of each lookback you made, and how many questions, summary cards and lookbacks you asked for.

Safety records. Reports you send, reports about your content, a copy of the reported content as the reporter saw it, the people you block, and account restrictions.

Notifications. If you allow notifications, your device’s push token and platform.

Promotion taps. When you open a link to one of our other apps, the app sends the app, link placement, platform and destination. The request uses your signed-in session. The daily aggregate table stores counts by those fields without account IDs or IP addresses.

Messages to us. If you email us, your email address and what you write.

Feedback. In-app feedback includes its type and text, language, platform, app version, receipt time and linked account.

Membership. When you buy or restore a membership, we receive and check the store’s proof of purchase: on the App Store a transaction signed by Apple, on Google Play a purchase token. From it we store the transaction identifier or purchase token, the product and plan, when it expires and when we last checked it, with your account, and for an App Store purchase also when it was bought. A Google Play purchase’s token and product are kept separately until Google has acknowledged the purchase. Apple tells our server when a subscription renews, expires or is refunded. For an App Store purchase that was refunded or revoked, we record the transaction identifier, when it was bought, and when the refund and its notice came. While your account exists this record stays linked to it, so that the membership can be given back if the refund is reversed; deleting your account removes the link. When you buy, the app passes the store an identifier made from your account ID. It looks random and is the same for every purchase from your account; it is not your account ID or email itself. Apple or Google handles payment details such as your card number, and we do not receive them. For a canceled Google Play lifetime purchase, we keep only a hash of the purchase token and the time we confirmed the cancellation, without an account link.

Focus keepsakes. The keepsakes you collect by finishing stretches with the focus timer. With your account we store which keepsakes you gathered and when each first came, and, for each focus place, the total focus time you finished there, the latest local date you finished a stretch there, the focus time you finished on that date, the number of days in a row, and, when needed to combine streaks across devices, which days you finished a stretch there in the seven days ending on that date. To check daily and streak keepsake eligibility across devices, we also store when you finished your latest counted stretch at each place and whether you had a membership when you started it. So that saves from several devices do not collide, we also store a random installation identifier the app creates on each device and a check value of the latest save. This lets you get your keepsakes back after reinstalling the app and see them on your other devices.

Shop rewards. We keep the age group you select (18 or younger, or 19 or older) and when you selected or last changed it. We do not ask for a birth date. We record daily reward dates and amounts, and for ad attempts, the account, session, ad unit, timing and a hashed Google transaction reference when credited.

On your device. Unsent drafts are stored on your device and are not submitted to the server until you send them. The focus timer’s record (time focused per day) and settings also stay on your device and are not sent to the server. Android backup rules exclude app data from automatic cloud backup and device transfer. On iOS, the folder holding drafts is marked as excluded from backup. This setting guides the OS and does not guarantee that backups or restores are prevented. Burning a draft does not erase copies in past backups. Your typewriter’s colour and model, its stickers and effects, the typing sound, the question switch and the view of the writing screen are also kept only on your device and are not sent to the server. So are the city names remembered for stickers (cities your letters travelled between); they stay after you delete a letter and are erased from that device when you delete your account in the app. Widgets receive only the following, on the device: the home screen widget gets the number of arrived letters, and the iOS Lock Screen widget gets the number of arrived letters, whether you have a membership, the name of the city a letter you sent is travelling to and its expected arrival time, and a running focus timer. Letter text and letter identifiers are never passed to a widget.

2. Approximate location

Moss needs to know roughly where letters travel from and to. Our hosting provider estimates a city from your IP address. We round coordinates to about 10 km and store the approximate city separately from security records. Other people see only a wider region name or the nearest major city in the same country. The app does not request or use device location services.

For sign-in security, temporary rate-limit records contain IP-based keys. A scheduled cleanup removes records more than two days after their last request; delays in that job can delay removal. Session records do not store IP addresses or User-Agent values. This does not mean our hosting provider keeps no network or security logs.

3. How we use information

We use this information to run the app: to sign you in, deliver letters between cities and time zones, pass letters only between people in the same age group while we have age-group delivery turned on, translate letters, review content for safety, make questions while you write letters to your future self, and a summary card or a lookback when you ask, show help resources when a letter suggests someone may need them, send notifications, handle reports and blocks, prevent abuse and rate limit requests, fix problems and answer your messages. We also use aggregate link counts to measure promotion of our own apps.

We use shop information to apply the age and daily limits, confirm ad completion, credit sparks and prevent abuse or duplicate rewards. We use membership information to confirm a membership purchase and provide its benefits, and to stop a refunded purchase from being used again. We use focus keepsakes to restore them after a reinstall and show them on your other devices.

4. AI review and translation

Before a letter or reply is delivered, we send its text, its language and, for replies, the original letter as context, to OpenAI. OpenAI checks it against our letter rules and translates it. AI never writes or edits your letters. On newer app versions, we ask for permission on the screen where you accept the Terms of Use, either during setup or when you review the updated agreement. Your permission applies to later letters, replies and revisions. Older versions ask for permission each time you send.

Letters to your future self. These letters go to no one else, so they are not reviewed or translated. We send them to the external AI service in two ways. First, questions come by themselves. The question switch on the writing screen is on by default. While it is on, when you stop at the end of a sentence or paragraph, we send what you have written so far, the questions that letter has already had and the app language to the external AI service, without a press from you, and get one question back. The questions it has already had are those printed on its paper and those it received that are no longer on the paper (for example ones you removed), five at most; they are sent so that the same question does not come twice. There are limits on how often this is sent for one letter and in one day (Terms, section 7). If you turn the switch off, nothing is sent for questions, for this letter and for later ones, until you turn it on again. This choice is saved on that device, so on another device or after reinstalling the app the switch starts on again. While we have turned off questions that come by themselves, the switch is a button instead, and the same items are sent only when you press it. Second, a summary card and a lookback are sent only when you ask. If you ask for a summary card for a letter that has come back, we send that letter’s text and get back up to three passages chosen from it. If you ask for a lookback (My week, My month or My year), we send the text and writing dates of the letters that came back in that period and get back passages chosen from them. Letters that have not come back yet are not sent. A new letter shows one general question printed on the paper. It comes from a list built into the app and is picked by your device’s date, with no AI involved. Picking and printing it sends nothing anywhere. This question counts among the questions the letter has already had, so it is sent with each later question, as described above. Every question is shown as if Moss asks it, in his way of speaking. The opening question comes from the list in the app and nothing is sent for it. The questions that come later while you write are generated by the external AI service from what you have written so far, and no person writes them. Until you send the letter, we do not store or log the text and question exchanged with the AI service while you write. When you send the letter, the questions printed on its paper are stored with it, shown again on the letter when it comes back, and deleted with the letter. We store summary cards and lookbacks with your letters, along with how many requests you made. Summary cards and lookbacks are made only from the letter’s own text, and the questions are never sent for them. If you turn the switch off and never ask for a summary card or a lookback, your letters are not sent to the AI service. Your consent to this processing is recorded together with your acceptance of the terms when you tap “I agree” on first start or when reviewing updated terms.

To withdraw consent, delete your account in Settings > Delete account or email jmjplearner@gmail.com. Once you withdraw, you can no longer send letters, replies or encouragement letters, because they require processing by the AI service.

Requests that have an option to store the response are sent with it turned off. According to OpenAI’s API policies, API data is not used to train their models by default, but OpenAI may keep it for a limited period to monitor abuse. See OpenAI’s Privacy Policy.

5. Service providers

We use a small number of providers to run the app:

  • Cloudflare hosts our server and database and estimates your city from your IP address.
  • OpenAI reviews and translates letters and replies. For letters to your future self, it makes questions from the text written so far while the question switch is on, and receives their text for a summary card or a lookback only when you ask for one.
  • Apple and Google provide sign-in. Apple Push Notification service (APNs) and Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) deliver notifications without letter text.
  • Google AdMob and its consent tool (UMP) may receive your IP address, an approximate location derived from it, app and device information (including available identifiers), ad views and interactions, and crash or performance data. Google uses this data to manage consent, serve and measure ads, and prevent fraud. We disable publisher first-party IDs. We send only an opaque attempt token as custom data to verify a reward. We do not send your app account ID, email or letter contents to ad providers. Google handles and retains SDK data under its Privacy Policy.
  • GitHub processes in-app feedback and letter or reply reports as issues in a private repository. Reports include the reason, content as the reporter saw it, content type, language, date and a report reference. We do not add account IDs or email addresses, though text may identify someone.
  • Apple (App Store) and Google (Google Play) process payment when you buy or restore a membership. They receive an identifier made from your account ID (not your account ID or email itself) and, for Google Play, the purchase token and product identifier. For a Google Play purchase, our server also talks to Google when it confirms and acknowledges the purchase or checks the subscription again. You give your payment method and payment details to the store directly, and they do not pass through us. Letter content is not sent. Nothing is sent unless you buy a membership.

On iOS, when you first choose to watch an eligible ad, Apple may ask for App Tracking Transparency (ATT) permission. If you allow tracking and Google's regional consent permits personalization, we request personalized ads. If tracking is denied, we request general ads when Google permits ad requests. If ATT and any other required consent allow it, Google may use the iOS advertising identifier (IDFA) to measure ads or limit how often you see them, including for general ads. On Android, the Personalized ads switch in Settings is off by default. On Android, we block the advertising ID and advertising-related AdServices permissions. If you select personalized ads, the request is subject to Google’s rules and the data available. Google may still serve general ads. When personalization is not allowed, we request general ads if Google’s consent tool permits requests. If it does not permit requests, we do not request ads.

These providers may process information in the United States and other countries. They handle it under their own terms and privacy policies.

6. How long we keep information

We keep your account and letters while your account exists. A letter travels for at most 14 days. If nobody replies, it comes back to you. You can burn a returned letter and its reply, which deletes their text, translations and review data and leaves only a minimal record that the letter existed. Letters to your future self (their text, the questions printed on the paper, when sent, the return day, when read, the summary card and the passages chosen for lookbacks) are kept until you delete the letter or your account. When you delete a letter, its text, its printed questions, its summary card and the lookback passages chosen from it are removed; a random identifier and the day it was sent stay until you delete your account, so the same letter is not sent twice. We keep the kind and period of each lookback made until you delete your account, and a scheduled job deletes the counts of requests for questions, summary cards and lookbacks after 7 days.

When you delete your account, we delete the account, sessions, your letters and replies you wrote. We revoke the Apple sign-in tokens available to us and guide you if you need to remove Apple access manually. We remove linked private GitHub feedback asynchronously within 7 days; deletion is complete only after that cleanup. To review safety issues, we retain reports you filed or received, including their reason, reported content and handling status, without user IDs for up to 90 days, then delete them. The text may still identify someone. Membership information (product, transaction identifier or purchase token, expiry) and focus keepsakes are kept until you delete your account and are deleted with it. The transaction identifier and time of a refunded or revoked App Store purchase are kept for 3 years, to stop a refunded purchase from being used again and to give the membership back if the refund is reversed. They stay linked to your account while it exists and are kept without account information after you delete it. For a canceled Google Play lifetime purchase, the hash of the purchase token and the cancellation confirmation time are kept for 3 years from the latest confirmation, also without account information and after account deletion. Cloudflare D1 recovery history may retain deleted data for up to 30 days, depending on the plan. Other provider records follow their own retention rules.

After delivery, we clear feedback text from our Cloudflare D1 database. We delete the raw feedback issue and related copies no later than 90 days after it is closed or 180 days after receipt, whichever comes first. Unresolved feedback is deleted within 180 days of receipt.

We delete GitHub report issues and related copies by the earliest of 180 days after receipt, 90 days after the issue is closed or the report is resolved by an administrator, or 90 days after account deletion. This deadline applies to GitHub copies; the safety records described above follow their own retention period.

After a deletion request is completed, we keep verification evidence and request metadata until the earlier of 90 days after completion or 180 days after receipt. For unfinished requests, we retain the minimum needed to complete the work.

Your ad choice is stored on this device for your account. We keep the current age group, when it was set, and awarded reward records until account deletion. A scheduled cleanup removes ad attempt records after one day has passed since the applicable daily reward window closed; account deletion also removes them. Google’s own data follows its retention rules.

7. Your choices and rights

You can delete your account in Settings > Delete account. You can turn notifications off in your device settings. You can block anyone who writes to you.

The app has no way to change a saved age group. To correct it, ask as described in section 7. On Android, you can turn personalized ads on or off in the app’s Settings. When personalization is not permitted, general ads may be requested. Where available, Ad privacy choices manages Google’s regional consent separately. On iOS, you can change the separate ATT permission in the device’s Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking. You can exchange letters without watching ads.

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or export your information, or to object to or restrict how we use it. To make a request, email jmjplearner@gmail.com. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

8. Children

The app is not intended for children under 14. If you believe a child under that age is using the app, contact us and we will delete the account. The app does not ask your age or birth date when you sign up; it asks only the age group described in section 1.

We do not request ads or load ad consent forms for accounts that report being 18 or younger.

9. Security

Data is sent over HTTPS, access to our systems is limited, and our logs record error codes, not letter text or account IDs. No service can be perfectly secure, so please don’t put identifying information in your letters.

10. This website

This website, including the page where this policy is published, uses Google Analytics 4 to understand visits. Google may receive browser and device information, an approximate location from your IP address, the pages you view and cookies or similar identifiers, under Google’s Privacy Policy. The app itself does not use Google Analytics.

11. Changes

If we change this policy, we will update the effective date above. If the change matters, we will tell you in the app before it takes effect.
